# The Free Website / Revenue Share Pitch

The offer: we build the site for free, and we get paid a percentage of the money the site
makes. This document is the deal structure first, the scripts second — because the scripts
only work if the deal underneath them is real.

Voice per `brand-ethos`: sharp, not loud. Partner with skin in the game, never a vendor.

---

## 1. The rule that makes this work

**Only take a percentage of money that moves through something you control.**

If you can't see the transaction in a dashboard you own, you cannot bill on it, and chasing
it will kill the relationship by month two. So:

| Money type | How you get paid |
|---|---|
| Checkout / booking / deposit you built and host | **Percentage.** It's in your Stripe. It settles itself. |
| Walk-ins, cash, phone calls, DMs | **Flat fee or per-lead**, never a percentage |
| Ongoing hosting, changes, support | **Monthly retainer** once the site is live |

This is the whole game. It converts "give me a cut of your sales" — which sounds like you
want to marry into their business — into "I take a cut of the checkout I built you," which
is obvious, auditable, and boring. Boring is what gets signed.

## 2. The default deal

**Build: $0.** Design, build, deploy, domain wiring, mobile, the whole thing.

**Then one of two ways to pay:**

- **Option A — Partner.** 10% of everything that runs through the site's checkout or
  booking system, for 24 months. No build cost, no monthly. You only pay when it works.
- **Option B — Buy it out.** Any time in the first 90 days, pay a flat $X and the rev share
  disappears. Site is yours outright.

Always present both. Option B is what makes Option A safe — it tells them they're not
signing away a piece of their business forever, they're renting your risk. Most people
pick A. The ones who blow up pick B, and you get a lump sum from a winner. Either way
you win, and saying that out loud is what makes you sound like a partner instead of
someone angling.

**Fine print worth having, in plain language:**
- Percentage applies to gross sales through the site, minus refunds and processing fees.
- Payouts monthly, automatic, from the platform — they never write you a check.
- They can walk any time. If they leave before 90 days, they buy it out at the flat rate;
  after that, rev share ends and the site is theirs.
- You keep it in your portfolio and a small credit in the footer. (Cheap for them, and
  it's your lead engine.)

## 3. Who to pitch

The offer only makes sense for people who **have something to sell online but currently
can't take money online.** Rank prospects by that, not by who's friendliest:

- Best: services with deposits — photographers, barbers, detailers, trainers, caterers,
  contractors, event people, tattoo artists. High ticket, obvious booking flow.
- Good: anyone with an Instagram they're selling from via DMs. The DMs *are* the pitch —
  you're removing them.
- Skip: businesses whose money is all walk-in cash, and anyone who already has a working
  checkout. Nothing for a percentage to attach to.

## 4. The scripts

Same four beats every time: **Observation → Offer → Catch → Close.** Never lead with
"free" — free reads as worthless or as a trap. Lead with the specific thing you noticed.

### The cold DM / short message

> Hey — saw you're taking bookings through DMs. I build sites for people in your line of
> work and I'll build yours for free. No catch on the build, I don't charge for it.
>
> How I make money: I put a real booking/checkout on it, and I take 10% of what comes
> through that page. If nothing comes through, I make nothing. If you'd rather just own it
> outright, you can buy it out flat any time in the first 90 days.
>
> Want me to put together a first version so you can see it? Costs you nothing to look.

Three sentences of substance, one ask. The ask is *to look at something*, not to sign.

### The in-person / phone version

**Observation:**
> How are people booking you right now? … Okay so it's all DMs and texts. How many of
> those do you think you lose because you didn't get back fast enough?

Let them answer. They'll name a number, and that number is now their number, not yours.

**Offer:**
> Here's what I do. I build the site — free. I'm not going to charge you to build it and
> I'm not going to sell you hosting. It'll take deposits, book people in, run on their
> phones.

**Catch (say it before they ask — this is the whole pitch):**
> The way I get paid is I take a cut, 10%, of the money that comes through that booking
> page. Not your walk-ins, not your cash, not the stuff you already have — I have no
> business touching that. Only the new stuff the site brings in, that goes through the
> checkout I build. If the page doesn't make you money, I don't get paid. That's on
> purpose. It means I'm not going to build you something pretty that doesn't work.

**Close:**
> Give me a week. I'll build the first version and show you. If you hate it, you throw it
> away and you've lost nothing. If you like it we turn it on and see what it does.

### The follow-up (no answer, 4–5 days later)

> Following up on this once and then I'll leave it alone — the offer's the same, I build
> it, you pay nothing up front, I make money only if it does. If it's not the right time
> just say so and I'll check back in a few months.

Say "and then I'll leave it alone" and actually mean it. It converts better than three
more messages, and it keeps the door open.

## 5. Objections

**"What's the catch?"**
> The catch is I'm betting on you. I make nothing unless the site makes money, so I'm
> picky about who I do this for. I think yours will work.

**"10% forever? That's a lot."**
> It's 10% of new money that didn't exist before, for 24 months, and only on what runs
> through the page. And you can buy me out flat in the first 90 days if you'd rather own
> it clean. Most people who do well take that option — that's fine by me.

**"How do I know you'll actually finish it?"**
> You don't pay me anything until it's live and working, so an unfinished site pays me
> zero. Here's what I've built — [link]. Give me a week and judge the first version.

**"I already have a website."**
> Can people pay you on it right now, in under a minute, on their phone? … Then you have
> a brochure, not a store. I'll build the part that takes money and leave the rest alone.

**"My cousin/nephew does websites."**
> Good — have them build it. I'm not selling a website, I'm taking a position in the part
> that makes money. If they'll do that, take their deal.

**"Let me think about it."**
> Totally. Can I build the first version while you think? Doesn't obligate you to
> anything and you'll be deciding on something real instead of an idea.

## 6. What you send after they say yes

Keep this tight — anything heavier than one page kills momentum:

1. **One-page agreement.** Scope, the 10%, the 24 months, the buyout number, the
   cancel-any-time clause. Plain English, no lawyer voice.
2. **Five questions**, no more: logo/photos, services and prices, deposit amount, where
   payouts go, domain (own it or need one).
3. **A date.** "First version by [day]." Then hit it.

Then build it, put the checkout on it, and let the first payout do the selling for the
next client.

---

## 7. What to watch for

- **Don't do more than 2–3 of these at once.** Rev share means unpaid build time up front;
  three stalled builds is a month of free work.
- **Cap the free build.** Landing page, booking/checkout, mobile. Anything past that is
  scoped and paid. Say this out loud during the build, not after.
- **The domain and the account go in their name, your access.** Never hold a client's
  business hostage on infrastructure — it's the fastest way to become a vendor they resent.
- **Track the ones that fail.** A dead site is data about which verticals to stop pitching.
