# Austin property manager outreach

A prospect list for the short-term rental and multifamily photography service,
and the reasoning behind the order. Companion file:
`austin-property-managers.csv`.

## Why this list exists

The Austin STR microsite's own economics say the organic traffic is small — the
search volume in this niche is low enough that Google Trends does not register
`"airbnb photographer"` at the Texas level. What the numbers *do* support is the
tail: one property manager relationship is worth more than the entire
projected organic return.

A single STR management company with forty listings is forty potential shoots,
and it onboards new properties continuously. That is the whole argument for
picking up the phone rather than waiting for Google.

## The order, and why

**P1 — short-term rental managers.** These are the highest-leverage targets and
they are not really clients; they are a channel. They control the listing, they
onboard properties continuously, and every new property they take on needs
photographs before it goes live. One relationship compounds.

HostStarter is the single best target on the list because they publicly handle
the city licensing process for their managed properties. They are already deep
in the July 1 2026 deadline with their clients, which means they are touching
every listing they manage. That is the moment photographs get revisited — and
Austin's new occupancy formula, two guests per bedroom plus two capped at ten,
means a lot of listings now advertise a capacity their photos and copy no longer
match. There is a page on the microsite about exactly this to send them.

**P2 — mid-size Austin multifamily.** AMP, ATX and PMI are the sweet spot: large
enough that portfolio consistency is a genuine problem worth paying to solve,
small enough that you can reach someone who can say yes. AMP's 45 communities is
the `$850` property package repeated forty-five times, or one `$1,600/mo`
retainer.

**P3 — smaller local multifamily.** Lower ceiling, shorter path to a decision.

**P4–P5 — large and national.** Evernest (22,000+ units) and FirstService have
the biggest portfolios and the longest sales cycles, usually with procurement in
between. Worth one attempt each. Not worth a campaign.

## What to lead with

Different pitch per segment. Do not send the same email to both.

**To an STR manager** — you are offering to make their onboarding easier and
their owners happier. Flat rates by bedroom count so they can quote an owner
without calling you, 24–72 hour turnaround so a property is not sitting unlisted,
and full rights so the images follow the property. Point at
`/short-term-rental-photography/` and, for anyone mid-licensing,
`/austin-str-license-and-your-listing/`.

**To a multifamily manager** — you are offering consistency across a portfolio.
Eight communities shot by eight local photographers look like eight unrelated
buildings on their own website. Point at `/property-managers/`, which carries the
per-unit, per-property and retainer scopes.

## Before you send anything

1. **The site has no photographs on it.** Sending a photographer's website with
   no photographs on it is worse than sending nothing. Shoot two or three real
   Austin properties first. This is the blocker for the whole list.
2. **Find a name.** Every row here is a company and a contact form, not a person.
   Ten minutes on LinkedIn per company turns a form submission into an email to
   someone who can decide. The form is the fallback, not the plan.
3. **Verify before sending.** These were checked once, on the date below.
   Companies get acquired and sites move.

## Provenance

Every company here was found through public search and had its website confirmed
live before being added. Scale figures — unit counts, assets under management —
come from the companies' own public marketing, not from independent
verification, and should be treated as claims rather than facts.

No personal contact details are recorded. Each row points at a public business
contact route only. If you build this into a mail-merge, the responsibility for
CAN-SPAM compliance — real identity, a working unsubscribe, a physical address —
sits with the send, not with this file.

**Checked: 30 August 2026.** One company, HostStarter, returns 403 to automated
requests because of Cloudflare bot protection; the site is live in a browser.
